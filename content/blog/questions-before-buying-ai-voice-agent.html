Every AI voice agent demo sounds impressive. The voice is natural, the answers are quick, and the scripted caller always says exactly the right thing. Real callers don't.

Having spent a long time on the buying and selling side of contact centre technology, these are the questions I'd want answered before signing anything.

1. Can I hear it handle a messy call?

Ask to hear real calls, or test it yourself, and make it hard. Interrupt it. Change your mind halfway through. Give a postcode with a strong accent, on a bad mobile line, with a dog barking. Ask something it wasn't set up for. A good agent copes, or admits it can't help and passes you on. A poor one loops, guesses or keeps you stuck.

2. What happens when it can't help?

This matters more than almost anything else. Ask how a call gets to a person, how quickly, and what information goes with it. If your team picks up and has to ask the caller to start again, you've added a step, not removed one. Also ask what happens out of hours, when there's nobody to hand over to.

3. Will it work with our systems?

An AI that can only answer questions is useful. One that can actually book the appointment, update the record or check the order is far more useful. Ask which of your systems it connects to, whether that's already built or needs custom work, and who pays for and maintains that connection. Ask about your phone system too: how calls reach the AI, and whether you need to change provider.

4. Who builds it, and who looks after it?

Your opening times change. Your prices change. You launch a new service. Someone has to update the agent each time. Find out whether that's you, the vendor or a partner, how long changes take, and what they cost. Ask how you'll see what the AI is doing: call recordings, transcripts, and reports on what it handled and what it passed on.

5. How is it priced, really?

Most platforms charge for usage, often per minute or per conversation, plus setup. Get the full picture: setup and build, integration work, the usage rate, any minimum commitment, and what changes cost later. Then model it against your own call volumes, including your busiest month, not an average.

6. Where does the data go, and how do we get out?

Your callers will give it names, dates of birth, addresses and sometimes health or payment details. Ask where calls and transcripts are stored, for how long, who can access them, and whether a proper data processing agreement is in place under UK GDPR. And ask what happens at the end: the notice period, and whether you can take your call data and configuration with you.

7. Does it tell you how to improve?

No agent knows everything on day one. Callers will ask things nobody thought to set it up for, and the best agents get better because they learn from those gaps. Ask whether it flags the questions it couldn't answer, the calls it had to pass on and the points where callers got confused or gave up. Then ask whether it turns that into clear recommendations: a missing answer to add, a policy to clarify, a new task it could take on. A good agent tells you where it's falling short. A poor one quietly fails the same callers in the same way, month after month, and you only find out from complaints.

The short version

Don't judge on the demo. Judge on how it handles your hardest calls, how cleanly it hands over to your team, whether it keeps getting better, and what it will really cost to run for a year.
