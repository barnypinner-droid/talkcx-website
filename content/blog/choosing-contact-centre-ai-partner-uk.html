Most UK contact centres looking at AI voice agents end up with the same question: do we buy straight from the platform, or work with a partner who builds and runs it for us? Both routes can work. The right one depends on your size, your team and how much change you can take on at once.

A quick disclosure first: TalkCX is a PolyAI reseller partner, so we have a view. This guide tries to set out both sides fairly, so you can decide for yourself.

Going direct to the vendor

Buying direct makes the most sense when you have the people to own it. That usually means a team who can write and test conversations, someone who looks after your phone system and integrations, and the time to keep improving the agent after launch.

The upside is a direct line to the people who build the product and one contract instead of two. The catch is that larger vendors tend to focus their own teams on larger contact centres. If you run 15 or 50 seats, check how much hands-on help you will really get, and what it costs.

Working with a partner

A partner sits between you and the platform. A good one helps you decide what the AI should handle, designs and builds the agent, connects it to your phone system and CRM, and keeps tuning it once it's live. You get the same technology, with people whose job is to make it work in your contact centre.

This suits contact centres without a spare project team, or ones that want to start small and grow. The risks are a partner who adds cost without adding much, or one who only knows a single product. Ask what they actually do and how they charge.

Questions to ask either way

  • Who builds and changes the agent? Your opening hours and policies will change. Find out who updates the agent, how quickly, and what it costs.
  • How does it connect to our systems? Ask about your phone platform and CRM by name, and whether that connection already exists or needs building.
  • What happens when it needs a person? Ask how calls reach your team and whether the caller's details go with them.
  • How is it priced? Get setup, usage, minimum commitments and the cost of changes in writing, then test it against your busiest month.
  • Where does the data live? Ask where recordings and transcripts are stored, for how long, and for a data processing agreement under UK GDPR.
  • Who supports it after go-live? Ask who you call when something goes wrong, and who looks at the calls it couldn't handle.

Signs of a good partner

They tell you up front which vendors they partner with. They start with your calls and your goals rather than a demo. They are happy to say when AI isn't the answer, for example when a simpler IVR or process change would do. They can show you how they will measure success, and they stay involved after launch rather than handing over and disappearing.

Signs it isn't the right fit

Pricing that's hard to pin down, no clear answer on who maintains the agent, pressure to sign before you've heard it handle your own calls, or a long build before you see anything working. A sensible first step is small: one or two call types, measured properly, then grow from there.

Where to start

If you're not sure which route fits, start by looking at your calls. Which types come in most, which are simple enough for AI, and which need a person.
